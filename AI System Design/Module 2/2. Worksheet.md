## Scenario Options

### Scenario A — HealthBridge: Clinical Knowledge System

HealthBridge is a regional hospital network (8 hospitals, 40 outpatient clinics). Clinical staff — physicians, nurses, pharmacists — currently look up clinical guidelines, drug information, and care protocols by searching across a mix of licensed reference databases, internal SharePoint folders, and a drug formulary spreadsheet maintained by the pharmacy team. The search is slow, inconsistent, and frequently returns outdated results. Leadership wants an AI system that lets clinical staff ask questions in natural language and receive accurate, sourced answers drawn from authoritative clinical content.

The corpus: internal HealthBridge clinical protocols (~2,400 documents), a drug formulary (~8,000 entries), licensed clinical reference content (~50,000 articles), and selected peer-reviewed literature (~3,000 papers). Formats: PDF, Word, HTML, structured JSON. HIPAA applies — while the corpus itself is reference material rather than patient records, queries submitted by clinicians may contain patient context. All data must stay on HealthBridge infrastructure.

### Scenario B — LexAI: Legal Research Assistant

Meridian & Partners is a 200-attorney law firm specialising in commercial litigation and corporate law. Attorneys currently research precedents by manually searching Westlaw and their internal document management system (iManage), which holds 10 years of case files (~2 million documents). Research takes 3–6 hours per matter. Leadership wants an AI system that lets attorneys ask natural-language research questions — "what's our precedent for challenging arbitration clauses in employment contracts?" — and receive a synthesised answer with citations.

The corpus: 10 years of internal case files (briefs, memos, correspondence, transcripts), internal knowledge memos written by senior attorneys, and a curated library of external case law loaded from Westlaw exports. Attorney-client privilege applies: the corpus contains confidential client matter information. Data must never leave the firm's controlled infrastructure, and the system must never surface one client's information to an attorney working on a different matter.

### Scenario C — RetailMind: Conversational Product Search

ShopSphere is a consumer e-commerce platform with 15 million registered users and a catalogue of 8 million SKUs across 50+ product categories. Their current search is keyword-based. Users who type "birthday gift for a 7-year-old who likes dinosaurs under $40" get keyword matches on "dinosaur" — not a curated set of relevant products. Leadership wants a conversational search experience where natural-language queries are understood and mapped to relevant products, with contextual follow-up ("make it blue" should refine the previous results).

The corpus for retrieval: product titles, descriptions, attributes, and customer reviews for 8 million SKUs, updated continuously as the catalogue changes. The retrieval system feeds a structured query layer — it does not replace the existing Elasticsearch infrastructure; it enhances it. Latency is a hard constraint: the AI parsing layer must complete within 300ms at P95.
