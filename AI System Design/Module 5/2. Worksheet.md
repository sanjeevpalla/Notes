# Governance Architecture Review

**Time:** 3–4 hours  
**Format:** Written review (~2 pages)

---

## The Task

Choose one of the three scenarios below. Produce a governance architecture review for the AI system described. Your review should be specific enough that an engineering team could implement the recommendations. Generic risk lists and vague mitigation advice will not score well against the rubric. Do not read the model answer before completing your own.

---

## Scenario Options

### Scenario A — HealthBridge: Clinical Documentation AI

HealthBridge has deployed a clinical documentation AI (from Modules 3 and 4) that generates SOAP-format note drafts from physician-patient encounter transcripts. Physicians review and approve drafts before they are written to the EHR (Epic). The system processes 2,000 encounters per day, growing to 10,000 per day within 18 months.

Current state: the system is technically functional. It generates notes, physicians review them, and the approval triggers the Epic write. What it lacks: a formal governance architecture. There is no documented risk inventory. The guardrail design is a system prompt that says "only use information from the transcript." Logging captures latency and errors but not what the model was sent or what it said. There is no audit trail for which physician approved which note. The compliance team has asked for a governance review before the system expands beyond the pilot.

### Scenario B — LexAI: Legal Research and Drafting AI

Meridian & Partners has deployed the LexAI research and drafting system (from Modules 3 and 4). Attorneys use it daily for case research and first-draft documents. The system queries 10 years of internal case files and drafts contracts, motions, and correspondence. Current usage: 200 attorneys, ~50,000 research queries and ~5,000 drafting tasks per month.

Current state: the system works. Attorneys are broadly satisfied. What it lacks: a formal governance architecture. Risk has not been formally assessed. The only guardrail is a system prompt instruction not to surface information from one matter in responses for a different matter. There is no audit trail of which attorney queried what, and for which client matter. The managing partner has raised concerns after reading a bar association ethics opinion on AI and confidentiality, and has asked for a governance review before the firm rolls out to all practice groups.

### Scenario C — RetailMind: Conversational Search and Shopping Assistant

ShopSphere has deployed the conversational search and shopping assistant (from Modules 3 and 4). The system handles 2 million daily active users. Conversational search parses natural-language queries and returns product results. The shopping assistant guides users through higher-consideration purchases over multiple conversational turns.

Current state: both features are live. Product metrics are strong. What it lacks: a governance architecture appropriate for a system operating at scale with EU users. GDPR compliance has not been formally assessed for the AI layer. There are no documented guardrails — the shopping assistant can and occasionally does state incorrect product prices or availability from training data rather than live catalogue data. The legal team has flagged GDPR exposure and asked for a governance review.

|---|---|---|
| Risk specificity | Each risk is specific to this system — could not be copy-pasted into any AI system review | |
| Guardrail inline vs. async | Distinction is made and justified — not all inline, not all async | |
| Audit design completeness | Retention period, tamper-evidence decision, and access controls all addressed | |

| Regulatory translation | Each regulation is translated into a named architectural constraint — not just cited | |

**Overall quality signal:** If a regulator reviewed this document, would they find a specific, implementable governance architecture — or a list of good intentions? The difference is specificity: named tools, named thresholds, named retention periods, named access controls.
