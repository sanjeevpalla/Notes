# Capstone — AI Architecture Design Proposal

**Time:** 15–20 hours (the largest deliverable in the course — expect to spread this across several sessions)
**Format:** Single document (~9–11 pages, PDF recommended, diagrams embedded or as an appendix) + a short recorded presentation (10–15 minutes)

## The Task

This is the synthesis of the entire course. Choose one of the three scenarios below and produce a complete AI architecture proposal, as if presenting to that company's technology leadership team for approval. All three scenarios are written to require every one of the seven modules' frameworks — this isn't a bigger version of one module's exercise, it's all seven applied to one system.

This is not a theoretical exercise. The proposal must be specific enough that an engineering team could begin implementation from it. Vague recommendations and generic architecture diagrams will not score well against the rubric. Do not look at the model answer before completing your own.

## Scenario Options

### Scenario A — HealthBridge

**Company:** HealthBridge is a regional hospital network with 8 hospitals and 40 outpatient clinics. They serve approximately 2 million patients annually.

**Request:** HealthBridge wants to deploy an AI clinical documentation assistant. The system should listen to physician-patient encounters (with consent), understand the clinical context, and auto-draft clinical notes in SOAP format for the physician to review and approve before they are written to the EHR (Epic).

**Constraints and context:**
- All patient data is PHI — subject to HIPAA; data cannot leave the HealthBridge private cloud
- Physicians are time-pressured and resistant to technology that slows them down; latency is critical
- Clinical notes are legal documents; errors have serious consequences — physicians must review and sign off every note
- The EHR (Epic) has a documented integration API (FHIR-compliant)
- HealthBridge has an existing cloud infrastructure team but no ML engineering capability
- The CIO is skeptical about AI and will require evidence of safety controls before approving

**Scale:** 2,000 encounter sessions per day at launch; 10,000 per day within 18 months

### Scenario B — LexAI

**Company:** Meridian & Partners is a 200-attorney law firm specializing in commercial litigation and corporate law. They maintain 10 years of case files (approximately 2 million documents) in a document management system (iManage).

**Request:** The firm wants an internal AI system that can:
1. Search and synthesize across the 10 years of case files ("what's our precedent for this argument?")
2. Draft routine correspondence (engagement letters, status update emails, discovery requests) based on matter context
3. Flag risk clauses in incoming contracts for associate review

**Constraints and context:**
- Client confidentiality is paramount: AI must never surface one client's information to an attorney working on a different matter
- Attorneys are billable-hour-driven: any tool must demonstrably save time, not create it
- The iManage document management system has an API but has never been integrated with an AI system
- Some partners are enthusiastic; others are deeply skeptical of AI-generated legal content
- The firm's IT team is small (3 people) — operational complexity must be minimal

**Scale:** 200 attorneys as internal users; roughly 50,000 document searches per month

### Scenario C — RetailMind

**Company:** ShopSphere is a consumer e-commerce platform with 15 million registered users and 2 million daily active users. They sell across 50+ product categories.

**Request:** ShopSphere wants to replace their rule-based recommendation engine with a modern AI system covering three capabilities:
1. Personalized product discovery (the homepage "recommended for you" feed)
2. Conversational search ("I need a birthday gift for a 7-year-old who likes dinosaurs, under $40")
3. A conversational shopping assistant (guided shopping experience for higher-consideration purchases)

**Constraints and context:**
- System must handle 2 million DAU at launch with seasonal spikes of 5× at peak (Black Friday, holidays)
- Recommendation quality directly drives revenue; the current rule-based engine generates 35% of all purchases
- User data is rich (purchase history, browsing history, wish lists, reviews) but privacy-sensitive (GDPR compliance required)
- The product catalog has 8 million SKUs with frequent additions and price changes
- The engineering team is strong (50+ engineers) but has no prior AI system experience
- Time to launch: 9 months
